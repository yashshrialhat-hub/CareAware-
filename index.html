Awareness programs and what they change

An awareness program works in four steps. Each program below follows the same path.

Inform

Clear facts reach people through schools, media and health workers.

Change habits

People adopt safer behaviors like handwashing and balanced diets.

Detect early

Screening and check-ups catch disease when it is easiest to treat.

Lower burden

Fewer illnesses, lower costs and longer healthy lives.

Immunization drives

Campaigns that explain vaccine safety and bring vaccines to every door, especially remote areas.

Impact: eradicated smallpox and brought polio close to elimination.

Tobacco control

Warning labels, public education and smoke-free spaces that reduce smoking and secondhand exposure.

Impact: lower smoking rates and fewer heart and lung diseases over time.

Hygiene and sanitation

Handwashing, clean water and safe sanitation education in schools and villages.

Impact: fewer diarrheal and infectious diseases, especially in children.

Nutrition and obesity prevention

Programs that teach balanced meals, portion control and daily activity.

Impact: lower risk of diabetes, high blood pressure and heart disease.

Cancer screening

Awareness of warning signs and free screening camps for breast, cervical and oral cancers.

Impact: earlier diagnosis, better survival and lower treatment costs.

Mental health awareness

Campaigns that reduce stigma and show people where to find help.

Impact: more people seek support sooner, and workplaces and schools respond better.

Facts above are drawn from WHO and Global Polio Eradication Initiative reports. Please check the latest figures before publishing them in a report.

Frequently asked questions

What is BMI?

Body Mass Index is your weight in kilograms divided by your height in meters squared. It is a quick screening measure, not a diagnosis.

Is BMI always accurate?

No. It does not separate muscle from fat, and it ignores age, diet and family history. Athletes and older adults can get misleading results.

How often should I get health screenings?

It depends on your age, sex and family history. Ask your doctor which tests you need and when.

How is the diet plan made?

We estimate your daily energy needs from your age, gender, height, weight and activity, then adjust them gently toward a healthy weight. The meals are general examples and not a medical prescription.

Do awareness programs really work?

Large campaigns such as vaccination drives have eradicated or nearly eliminated serious diseases. Impact is strongest when information is paired with easy access to services.
